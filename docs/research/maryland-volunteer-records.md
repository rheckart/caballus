# What Maryland actually requires of a volunteer attendance record

Research for #12. Freedom Hill Horse Rescue is at 7940 Flint Hill Rd, Owings, MD 20736 — **Calvert County** — EIN 20-1933165, 501(c)(3) since 2006, with **zero paid employees**. That last fact does more work below than it looks like it should.

Confidence is marked throughout. **High** means the statute, regulation or form was read directly. This is research, not legal advice, and one item at the end is flagged for counsel.

> **An earlier pass on this question was scoped to Virginia and is void.** It is worth knowing why, because the conclusions inverted rather than shifted: Virginia has no statewide service requirement and a waiver-only equine statute; Maryland requires service hours to graduate and has no equine statute at all. Anything in this repo citing Va. Code § 3.2-6202, LCPS, SchooLinks or x2VOL is a fossil of that pass.

## Nobody requires a sign-in sheet

No Maryland or federal law requires the rescue to keep a volunteer roster, sign-in sheet, or hours log.

**MOSH does not reach the rescue at all.** Md. Labor & Employment § 5-101(c)(1) defines an employee as someone employed "for a wage or other compensation," and § 5-101(d)(1)(i) defines an employer as one who "employs at least one employee." Freedom Hill has none. Both predicates fail independently, so the federal-style evacuation-accounting rule — 29 CFR 1910.38(c)(4), "procedures to account for all employees after evacuation" — has nothing to attach to. _(High.)_

**Md. CJP § 5-407**, the Maryland Volunteer Service Act, protects a 501(c)(3)'s volunteers from damages beyond their own personal insurance, absent gross negligence or wilful misconduct, and imposes no record-keeping condition. **Md. CJP § 5-406** caps organisational liability but conditions that on _carrying insurance_ at ≥$200k per claim / $500k per occurrence — on buying a policy, not on filing paperwork. The federal Volunteer Protection Act (42 U.S.C. § 14503) protects volunteers only, never the organisation, and turns on the volunteer "acting within the scope of the volunteer's responsibilities" — a factual question that assignment records help answer, which is evidentiary rather than mandatory. _(High.)_

**Federal grant documentation** sets a standard of "verifiable in the recipient's records" (2 CFR 200.306(b)(1)) with a three-year retention floor (200.334) — no signature or supervisor-certification requirement, the 2014 Uniform Guidance having deliberately removed the old signed certification. **Form 990 line 6** asks for a volunteer headcount and its instructions expressly permit "a reasonable estimate… on any reasonable basis"; hours are optional Schedule O narrative. _(High.)_

**Under GAAP the hours cannot be booked as revenue.** ASC 958-605-25-16 recognises contributed services only where they create or enhance a nonfinancial asset, or require specialised skills that would otherwise be purchased. Feeding, mucking and turnout are neither. A donated vet's or farrier's time is; a volunteer's Saturday is not. _(High.)_

## What does bind, and who binds it

**The insurer's roster is a service commitment, not a coverage trigger.** CIMA's published VIS commitment requires the member to "accurately report the number of volunteers utilized by your organization" and to "maintain a roster of all volunteers." Its terms state coverage "becomes effective at the time of your **formal enrollment** in the volunteer program" — so an enrolment date genuinely matters. But the word _roster_ appears nowhere in the coverage terms themselves, which are written to the class ("all registered volunteers (collectively)"), and premium is rated on **peak simultaneous headcount**, one integer a year. CIMA's own published Sample Volunteer Roster is a _monthly grid_: last/first name, phone, ID code, dates worked this month, total hours this month. **There is no initial-service-date column and no termination-date column** — a claim to the contrary circulated in an earlier pass and was wrong. At claim time the coordinator must verify the claimant "is an agency volunteer (listed on an official agency volunteer roster) who was volunteering on the date and time of injury." _(High.)_

That claims sentence is the strongest genuine justification for the whole feature, and it maps exactly onto timestamped shift participation.

**Markel's equine liability application carries the hardest record obligation found anywhere**, as an underwriting representation: _"Is the signed release kept on file for a minimum of 5 years?"_ A dated, per-person artefact with a retention clock. Worth noting alongside it that the same form's coverage grant names only "the applicant, employees and independents" — the word _volunteer_ does not appear in it. For an organisation running sixty volunteers that potential gap is a broker question more consequential than any record-keeping one. _(High, conditional on Markel being the carrier — unconfirmed.)_

**Calvert County Public Schools requires pre-approved service-learning sites to "maintain liability insurance."** A counterparty condition of hosting students, and the one insurance requirement that binds today. _(Medium.)_

## Service learning is a graduation requirement

This is the finding that inverts the Virginia pass. **COMAR 13A.03.02.05.D**, verbatim:

> "Students shall complete one of the following: (1) Seventy-five hours of student service that includes preparation, action, and reflection components and that, at the discretion of the local school system, may begin during the middle grades; or (2) A locally-designed program in student service that has been approved by the State Superintendent of Schools."

The locally-designed alternative is not an escape hatch. MSDE's _Maryland Student Service-Learning Guidelines_ (February 2026): "all Maryland public agencies require students to complete a minimum of 75 hours." Charles County runs a locally designed programme and it is still 75 hours. Maryland was the first state in the nation to impose this. _(High.)_

Three consequences for the rescue:

**Reflection is the school's job, not the rescue's.** MSDE requires Academic Preparation, Service Action and Structured Reflection for every experience, and the still-operative 2002 guidelines say an activity done "without adequate preparation or reflection components as verified by designated school personnel… cannot be counted." The rescue supplies dates, hours, description of work and supervision. Building a reflection workflow would be building the school's half. _(High.)_

**Plain volunteering is not service learning by default.** MSDE states "volunteerism alone does not meet the criteria." A student who logs sixty barn hours nobody structured has sixty volunteer hours and zero SSL hours. The rescue's record is necessary and not sufficient, and a student should be told so in September rather than May. _(High.)_

**One hour of credit per hour of engagement.** No multipliers, no rounding up. MSDE's exclusions are service to one's own family, compensated service, work that replaces a paid staff member, revenue for a for-profit, court-ordered service, proselytising, and discriminatory activity. The rescue is clean on all of them — notably it has no paid staff to displace. _(High.)_

### The rule that shapes the software

MSDE, February 2026, verbatim and statewide:

> "A student's parent, guardian, or relative may not supervise or verify completion of an independent service-learning activity."

> "parents/guardians may not solely supervise or verify service-learning experiences. Designated school or community partner staff must supervise and/or verify service-learning experiences."

Set that beside the rescue's own published policy — children under 16 must be accompanied by a parent or guardian 18+ at all feed shifts — and the adult standing next to the student is frequently the one person who cannot sign for them. _(High.)_

No Maryland or county rule requires the attestor to be an officer, and none requires notarisation. Calvert asks for "a responsible adult representing a non-profit agency/organization" who directly supervised; Anne Arundel for "the organization's administrator or supervisor"; Prince George's additionally wants a printed title. _(High.)_

### The two feeder counties want different documents

**Calvert** — 75 hours, accruable grades 5 through 12. Four forms. The **Verification of Hours** form is "to be completed by project coordinator at approved Agency" and is a table of `Date | Hours Worked | Brief Description of Service | Signature of Site Supervisor`, one row per visit, then Total Hours, a final Site Supervisor Signature, Ending Date and Comments. The guidelines add that **"the adult supervisor signs off on the hours earned at each visit,"** cap credit at **8 SSL hours per 24-hour period**, and require adult supervision on site. The Project Description form wants the site supervisor's **name and phone**. Pre-approval — student, parent and SSL Coordinator, _before service_ — carries no agency signature at all. 260+ hours earns the MSDE Certificate of Meritorious Service in Calvert; Montgomery uses 240, the Maryland School for the Deaf 150, so the threshold is per-county. No published deadlines found. Paper, no online system found. _(High on the forms, Medium on the pre-approved-site list, which is held in guidance offices and is not published.)_

**Anne Arundel** — 75 total, of which 40 at high-school level, earned mostly _infused into required courses_. Independent hours are a gap-filler for students who missed infused opportunities through illness, transfer or home teaching — structurally the opposite of Calvert. The Project Agreement form records "Date(s) of Activity" and a total with **no per-date line items**, and takes four signatures: student/guardian, community partner, AACPS sponsor, and the Office of Service Learning. Its Preparation phase can be satisfied by attending "a training/development event or **orientation** sponsored by AACPS or the Community Organization, proof of attendance required" — which the rescue's mandatory New Volunteer Orientation already does, if it issues proof. Portal: ServeAACPS. _(High on the form, which is however dated November 2015; Medium on whether the portal has replaced paper.)_

**Prince George's**, if a student ever comes from there, is the most demanding data point: hard deadlines of **Oct 15 / Jan 31 / Jul 15**, a log with a signature per row, an organisation block requiring supervisor name _and title_, student reflection prompts on the verification form itself, and both student and parent/guardian signatures.

**No county examined requires letterhead.** _(High for Calvert and Prince George's, Medium for Anne Arundel.)_

## Minors

**Written parental consent is a statutory condition, not a nicety.** Md. Labor & Employment § 3-203 disapplies the child-labour subtitle where the activity is outside the minor's school hours, is not manufacturing or mining, is not a hazardous occupation, **and** falls in a listed category — including "(ix) work that is performed as an unpaid volunteer in a charitable or nonprofit organization, **if… a parent of the minor or a person standing in the place of the parent consents in writing**." Maryland Department of Labor's Minor Fact Sheet (rev. 8/23) confirms it in plain language. So: no work permit required, and written consent is the condition on which that rests. Note the second condition too — service must be **outside the school hours set for that minor**. _(High.)_

**Farm work on a farm** is an independent route to the same exemption under § 3-203(4)(i), and it does not textually require written consent. Rely on (ix) and take the consent anyway; two routes are not a reason to use the weaker one.

**Nothing in Maryland law sets a minimum age, an adult-to-minor ratio, or requires background checks** for unpaid volunteers at a general nonprofit. Md. Family Law § 5-551 makes checks mandatory only at listed facilities — licensed child care, schools, foster care, juvenile facilities, camps, and "a recreation center or recreation program operated by… a private entity primarily serving minors" — and makes them permissive elsewhere. The Youth Camp Act (COMAR 10.16.06) triggers at seven or more unrelated campers across seven calendar days in a three-week period; weekly shifts and a monthly two-hour orientation do not come close, though a week-long summer children's programme would. **All three of the rescue's minor rules — age 13, horse experience, parent present under 16 — are its own policy choices, and good ones.** _(High; Medium-high on the negative for ratios, which is proving an absence.)_

## Liability, where Maryland is unusual twice

**Maryland has no equine activity liability act.** The full 2025 texts of the Courts & Judicial Proceedings, Agriculture and Natural Resources articles contain zero occurrences of "equine" or "horse"; Maryland is one of a very small number of states — commonly listed with California and New York — with no such statute. **There is therefore no statutory sign to post, no prescribed release language, and no safe harbour.** Anyone telling the rescue it must post statutory equine warning signage is repeating another state's law. _(High.)_

**Maryland enforces parental pre-injury waivers.** _BJ's Wholesale Club, Inc. v. Rosen_, 435 Md. 714 (2013), held 5–2 that a parent's pre-injury waiver of a minor's negligence claim does not violate public policy. This is the minority rule nationally and it runs in the rescue's favour, which makes the parent/guardian signature line on their release do more work here than it would in most states. _(High.)_

**Md. Nat. Res. §§ 5-1101–5-1109**, the Recreational Use statute, is the strongest protection available and it fits unusually well: § 5-1103 removes the duty to keep premises safe or warn of dangerous conditions for recreational or educational entrants, and § 5-1101(c) defines "educational purpose" to include "farm visitations for purposes of learning about the farming operation" and "organized visits to farms by school children, 4-H clubs, FFA clubs, and others as part of their educational programs" — squarely the scout-badge and open-house work. § 5-1101(b)(2)(iii) excludes from "charge" any "contributions in kind or services," which is a decent argument that accepting volunteer labour is not fee-defeating. Posting is permissive (§ 5-1107), never required. **The exception that matters: charging the entrant defeats it.** Whether the rescue charges for any programme is an open question and a consequential one. _(High on the statute.)_

**One new risk.** Md. CJP § 5-401.2 (SB 452, effective 1 October 2024) makes void and unenforceable any provision "relating to the use of a recreational facility" that limits liability for the facility's own negligence. "Recreational facility" is defined as a **commercial** recreational or athletic facility or amusement attraction. "Commercial" probably excludes a 501(c)(3) that charges nothing, but the statute is new and untested, the University of Maryland's own agricultural law programme has flagged that horse-riding and agritourism facilities might be swept in, and **the rescue's release is marked "Updated 2020," predating it entirely.** _(High on the statute, Low on its scope here.)_

> **This is the one item in this document that wants an hour of Maryland counsel**, together with whether Maryland's common-law charitable immunity survives and whether carrying insurance abrogates it. Everything else here is settled enough to design against.

Maryland is also a pure contributory-negligence jurisdiction, which independently favours the defence and raises the value of a contemporaneous record of what a volunteer was told and assigned.

## Two smaller reversals

**The fire code has no farm-building exemption.** COMAR 29.06.01.03 exempts only Baltimore City, buildings used solely as dwellings for not more than two families, and certain pre-existing conditions. Virginia exempts farm structures; Maryland does not. The _building_ code goes the other way — Md. Public Safety § 12-508 exempts agricultural buildings with agritourism as a subordinate use, and names Calvert County specifically. _(Medium-high; read via Cornell's reproduction of COMAR rather than the Department of State Documents original, and neither NFPA 150 adoption nor Calvert's local amendments were checked.)_

**The value of a volunteer hour in Maryland is $36.51** — Independent Sector, 2025 data, published 21 April 2026, against $36.14 nationally. Flagged as preliminary BLS data and subject to revision. It is derived from average hourly earnings for private non-farm production and non-supervisory workers, not animal-care wages. Legitimate for a board report or annual narrative; **not** a federal match rate, which wants local rates for similar work under 2 CFR 200.306(e), and not bookable as revenue. It moves every April, so store it as a dated table and state which year a report used. _(High.)_
