Real‑feel

The finding that matters most

The board is a lossy log, and it is already losing. Your first photos and these ones are the same board months apart. In the earlier set, Delilah's cell held six dated weights and Apollo's held four. Today every horse has two — the rest were wiped to make room. Delilah is gone from the board entirely, Apollo has moved from stall 7 to stall 10, and stall 7 now reads OPEN.

Nobody decided to throw that history away. The board ran out of space. The rescue is already trying to keep a weight history and physically cannot — which is a stronger argument for this app than anything in the brief.

How to read this

All eleven horses and their real feed, medical, field, groom, blanket and weight data are transcribed from your photos. Stall 7 is on the board as OPEN, so it is here too. Blue occupies stalls 2 and 3. Mystery and Nora are in the Small Barn section, which the board keeps separate with its own AM/PM sub‑table.

Colour is load‑bearing on purpose. On the board, ink colour is convention that drifted — nobody agreed red means medical, it just mostly does. Here the colour comes from the field, so the meaning holds by construction. Blue underline is the board's own NEW mark, and it stays.

Halter colour is a field, and four horses have two colours — River is red and black, Sir lime green and yellow. The discs are split accordingly. Sir's name plate omits his colour even though the Horse's Colors panel records it, so the same fact lives in two places and disagrees.

not on the board marks the one column I added: Lunch. The board has no lunch column, so Dawson's and Apollo's lunch is smuggled into ALERT and into the FEED/GRAIN panel's weekday duty roster.

Only two of the eleven horses get lunch — Dawson and Apollo. Everywhere lunch appears, it says so: the grid reads no lunch feeding rather than a dash, and switching the shift to Lunch narrows the queue to those two horses and nobody else.

The prep handoff is now visible. Switch to Lunch and look at Dawson: his soak is prepared during the AM shift, so the card says whether that actually happened. Tick Dawson's AM medical step, come back to Lunch, and it turns green. This is the failure you described — the AM leads missed Dawson's lunch prep and the lunchtime person only found out on arrival.

Elsewhere on the same board, not modelled here

The feed grid is one panel of about ten. These are the others, and most of them are v1 work that the map does not currently account for:

  • Reminders — barn‑wide standing rules. Take turns wide, no scissors in fields, scrub fly masks when dirty, check for wet spots when mucking.
  • Horse's Colors — halter colours again, duplicating the name plates.
  • Baths 2026 — a per‑horse date log with blanks for not‑yet‑done. A schedule, a history and an implied overdue, exactly as the brief predicted.
  • Feed/Grain — who may prepare grain and meds, and a weekday duty roster by name for Apollo's and Dawson's lunch.
  • Hay Amounts — per‑paddock quantities by receptacle, plus a whole alternate per‑horse hay plan that only applies when the heat rule fires.
  • Water — stall buckets, troughs, electrolytes, salt blocks.
  • Sheets and Blankets — the real threshold model, modelled below.
  • Maintenance — contacts plus an open need: T‑post leaning in A paddock.
  • Medicines & Supplements — product to supplier, with prescription flags.
  • Supplies Needed — a reorder log with dates and who was notified, and a days‑of‑supply table: Senior 14.5 days, Rice Bran 13.
  • Escalation contacts — three named people to text about horse care, the vet clinic with hours, an emergency line, the property owner.

Two more paper artifacts surface here that the map's list of four does not mention: a log book (River's cell says "Text Lori — note in Log book") and a printed Feed Shift Leaders closing checklist screwed to the wall below.
